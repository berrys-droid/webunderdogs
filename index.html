The problem

The shelf moved into the assistant.

Ask an assistant for a wine and it answers from what it has read: a few big publications, Wikipedia, retailer pages and whoever wrote clearly about the question. A family estate in Almansa or an importer with forty small producers often isn't on that reading list at all. And when it does mention you, it can be wrong: a grape you don't grow, a region you're not in.

Bain & Company found that about 60% of searches now end without a click to any website, and 80% of search users rely on AI summaries for at least 40% of their searches. Bain, Feb 2025. Not about wine, but it's where your buyers are headed.

  1. The assistants have to be able to read you. Their bots let in, your pages indexed, your facts on the page instead of inside a PDF.
  2. Your pages have to answer the question, answer first, in your buyers' words, with something only you could say.
  3. The sources they trust have to mention you: critics, retailers, listings and trade pages that agree about you.

The method

From baseline to before-and-after.

  1. Map the prompts

    Twenty questions your buyers really ask, built with you: swap, grape, region and where-to-buy. Then the set is frozen so the comparison stays fair.

  2. Take the baseline

    Four runs per prompt on ChatGPT, Google's AI answers, Gemini and Claude. Named, cited, position, every factual error, and whether the answer came from a search or from memory.

  3. Fix what they read

    Answer-first pages, trade sheets and listings that agree with each other, and real editorial mentions on sites the assistants already trust.

  4. Run it again

    Same prompts, same method, 28 days or more later. Before and after, per prompt and per assistant, including what didn't move.

On my own site, every Google citation in the baseline landed on a page built answer-first. The shape isn't enough on its own, though. One page with every item on that list was cited in none of its five runs.

Questions

Common questions

You work with Chris Berry directly: editor of Wine Underdogs, Rhetoric and Composition at UCF, years of teaching writing and translating. Most of GEO is saying a true thing so clearly that a reader, human or machine, repeats it correctly.

What is GEO?

Generative engine optimization is the work of getting a business named, cited and described correctly when AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity and Gemini answer a question.

How is it different from SEO?

For Google, it mostly isn't: Google says its AI answers run on the same index and quality systems as search, so sound SEO comes first. Two things are different. What you measure is a mention inside the answer, not a position in the links. And ChatGPT often answers from memory without searching at all, where no page fix reaches.

Which assistants do you measure?

ChatGPT, logged out, so it doesn't know who's asking, plus Google's AI answers, Gemini and Claude in signed-in sessions, and every report says which ran which way. Perplexity and Grok ask for an account before they'll answer, so they aren't measured yet, and the report says that too. Alongside the runs, with your read access, I add Google's count from Search Console and Microsoft's from Bing Webmaster Tools of how often your pages appear in their AI answers, each with its dates.

What's in the free check?

Three prompts I choose from what you sell, each run four times on ChatGPT while logged out, so it can't tailor the answer to me. You get whether you were named and cited in each run, who was named instead, and which sources it read. It's the first page of an AI Shelf Read, which covers 20 prompts on four assistants.

Can you guarantee results?

No, and be wary of anyone who does. Assistants change their answers between runs and between weeks. I guarantee the method: a frozen prompt set, four runs per assistant, the same conditions before and after, and a report that includes what didn't move.

Do I need this if I already rank on Google?

Ranking and being quoted are different things. In my own site's Google baseline, the page cited in all five AI answers for "wine like Barolo but cheaper" sat sixth to eighth in the links underneath. A top ranking doesn't put you in the answer, and a middling one doesn't keep you out.

Why a wine specialist?

Because the prompts are wine prompts. Knowing that buyers ask "what's like Barolo but cheaper", that Garnacha Tintorera and Alicante Bouschet are the same grape, or that a DOP has rules an assistant can misstate, decides which questions are worth winning and which errors matter.

Do you write about clients on Wine Underdogs?

Not as paid coverage. The two are kept apart, and if I ever write about a client's wine there, the piece says plainly that they're a client.

How long until I see a change?

The re-run happens at least 28 days after the baseline, because that's about the soonest a change to your pages shows up in the answers. Some changes take two or three cycles.

What won't you do?

Promise "always top three", because nobody can prove always; you get a measured proportion. Hide text or instructions for AI in your pages. Invent reviews, awards or mentions. Sell llms.txt, "AI files" or schema as an AI fix, since Google says its AI answers don't need them (I'll keep your structured data accurate for ordinary search results and claim nothing more for it). Build a page for every way to phrase a question, which Google treats as scaled content abuse. Or make health claims about wine.
