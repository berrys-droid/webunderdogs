The mission

The shelf moved into the assistant.

Ask an assistant for a wine and it answers from what it has read: a few big publications, Wikipedia, retailer pages and whoever wrote clearly about the question. The famous names fill that reading list. A family estate in Almansa or an importer with forty small producers often isn't on it at all.

And when it does mention you, it can be wrong. A grape you don't grow, a region you're not in. A confident wrong answer costs more than silence.

What decides it

Three questions put you in the answer, or leave you out.

01 · Access

Can the assistants read you?

Crawlers allowed, pages that load without a script, and facts in the page rather than locked in a PDF.

02 · Your pages

Do your pages answer the question?

The answer in the first sentence, in the words buyers use, marked up so a machine can lift it.

03 · Other people's pages

Do the sources they trust mention you?

Critics, retailers, listings and trade pages that name you, and say the same true things about you.

An AI Shelf Read checks all three, for you and for whoever the assistants name instead.

Answer engineering

Pages a machine can quote without rewriting.

The answer goes first, in a sentence an assistant can lift whole: what the wine is, what it tastes like, what it replaces, where to buy it. Then clean structured data, a trade sheet that matches your tech sheets, and a "common questions" block shaped like the questions buyers actually ask.

  • Answer-first copy on the pages your buyers' questions land on
  • FAQ and organization schema that matches what the page says
  • Your robots.txt checked, so ChatGPT, Google, Claude and Perplexity can read you
  • Tech sheets, trade sheets and listings that agree with each other

On my own site, every Google citation in the baseline landed on a page built this way. The shape isn't enough on its own, though: one page with every item on that list was cited in none of its five runs.

Source authority · accuracy watch

Earn the mention. Then keep it true.

Assistants trust sources other sources trust. I build real editorial mentions, correct your listings and trade profiles, and give the models a named author and named producers to cite.

  • No guarantee of "always top three." Nobody can prove always. You get a measured proportion.
  • No hidden text or instructions to AI. Whatever the machines read, your customers can read.
  • No invented reviews, awards or mentions. Not in front of a model, not in front of a person.
  • No health claims. Your marketing and your label approvals tell the same story.

Mission control

Common questions

You work with Chris Berry directly: editor of Wine Underdogs, Rhetoric and Composition at UCF, years of teaching writing and translating. Most of GEO is saying a true thing so clearly that a reader, human or machine, repeats it correctly.

What is GEO?

Generative engine optimization is the work of getting a business named, cited and described correctly when AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Google's AI Overviews, Perplexity and Gemini answer a question.

How is it different from SEO?

SEO aims at a position in a list of links. GEO aims at a mention inside a written answer, which often sits above that list and may be the only thing the buyer reads. A page can rank well and still never be quoted.

Which assistants do you measure?

ChatGPT, logged out, so it doesn't know who's asking, plus Google's AI answers, Gemini and Claude in signed-in sessions, and every report says which ran which way. Perplexity and Grok ask for an account before they'll answer, so they aren't measured yet, and the report says that too.

Can you guarantee results?

No, and be wary of anyone who does. Assistants change their answers between runs and between weeks. I guarantee the method: a frozen prompt set, four runs per assistant, the same conditions before and after, and a report that includes what didn't move.

Do I need this if I already rank on Google?

Ranking and being quoted are different things. In my own site's Google baseline, the page cited in all five AI answers for "wine like Barolo but cheaper" sat sixth to eighth in the links underneath. A top ranking doesn't put you in the answer, and a middling one doesn't keep you out.

Why a wine specialist?

Because the prompts are wine prompts. Knowing that buyers ask "what's like Barolo but cheaper", that Garnacha Tintorera and Alicante Bouschet are the same grape, or that a DOP has rules an assistant can misstate, decides which questions are worth winning and which errors matter.

Do you write about clients on Wine Underdogs?

Not as paid coverage. The two are kept apart, and if I ever write about a client's wine there, the piece says plainly that they're a client.

How long until I see a change?

The re-run happens at least 28 days after the baseline, because that's about the soonest a change to your pages shows up in the answers. Some changes take two or three cycles.
